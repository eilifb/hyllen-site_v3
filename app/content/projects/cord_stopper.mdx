### Background

Back in 2024 I pulled the plug and bought the pair of pants I had been eyeing for a solid while.
It was a pair of [Maharishi's](https://www.maharishistore.com/), specifically the Cordura Travel Pants (now unavailable, unfortunately).

Apart from being a high-quality pair of pants with a good fit, the thing that really sold me was the cords/straps.
A visual detail that wasn't too overstated but gave them some identity and, in my opinion, made for a nice nod to the pocket straps on the original paratrooper cargo pants from which these are derived.
However, while being the detail I liked the most, they also proved to be the most troublesome in use. Specifically, their length.

Aesthetically, I think the length is perfect, but when sitting lower to the floor, like the seats on my local metro, they end up dragging against the floor.
Not really an issue unless they end up under your shoes without you noticing when you stand up.
The stitches attaching the cords never really stood a chance. In my case I think it took about 20 minutes from unpacking the pants to stuffing the ripped cords into my pocket.

### Solution
The ability to adjust the length of a cord is not a new or niche need, and it has its solution in the cord stopper.

I could have used a 'normal' cord stopper or a bag clip like the ones pictured, or I could have spent some time scouring Amazon or AliExpress for a stopper intended for flat cords like the ones on my Maharishis.
But I already own a 3D printer, so I thought I might as well make something custom-fit. The design requirements were quite easy after all.
I wanted it to lay flat against the opening in the pocket for the cords, and I wanted it to have some sort of spring action to pinch the cords and keep them in place. Nothing really fancy.

The models were made in [Onshape](https://cad.onshape.com/documents/e47bebc9fb37d15b8fee76a0/w/815a611d750f4cec148d16bb/e/0095937ef959157ef914b546?renderMode=0&uiState=6a147200192f3790fd27ee4f).
I don't really know how sharing works in Onshape, but I think if you have an account you can fork the design from the link above. I will also try to get around to uploading them to [Thingiverse](https://www.thingiverse.com/).

There is probably a second revision out there that uses a better spring system than rubber bands. The rubber bands are kind of a "good enough" fix: they do not create much pinching force, so the cords come loose from time to time.
But the flat stopper 'plate' also redistributes some of the force that pulls on the attaching stitches onto the walls of the pocket if the cord snags on something.
At least I haven't had any issues with the cords ripping since attaching them, and adjusting the length from time to time isn't that big of a deal.
